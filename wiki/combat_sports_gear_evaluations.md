## 1. Biomechanical Impact Dynamics (183cm, 70.1kg User Profile)

### 1.1 Weight Class & Impact Physics
* **User Profile:** Height 183 cm (~6'0"), Weight 70.1 kg (~154.5 lbs).
* **Boxing Weight Class:** Super Welterweight / Light Middleweight (amateur Middleweight boundary).
* **Kinetic Impact Considerations:** At 183 cm with a long lever arm (reach), straight punches and hooks generate significant angular momentum. When striking a 40–60 kg dense heavy bag, decelerating that force creates tremendous compressive stress on the metacarpals, carpal bones, and the radiocarpal (wrist) joint.

### 1.2 Lace-Up vs. Velcro (Hook-and-Loop) Mechanics
* **The Failure Mode of Velcro:** Hook-and-loop straps only pull tension in one transverse direction across the back of the wrist, leaving the palm-side crease loose. When a hard hook impacts a heavy bag slightly off-center, the wrist joint can flex laterally or hyperextend, causing sprains or TFCC (triangular fibrocartilage complex) tears.
* **The Lace-Up Advantage:** A traditional lace-up closure provides a uniform, 360-degree cylindrical cast around the forearm and wrist. Pulling the laces cinches the segmented wrist cuff evenly from the base of the palm to several inches up the ulna/radius, effectively immobilizing the wrist in a neutral, straight alignment.

### 1.3 Glove Sizing: 14 oz vs. 16 oz for 70.1 kg (154.5 lbs)
* **14 oz Gloves:** Officially recommended by manufacturers (including Cleto Reyes) for fighters between 130 and 159 lbs. Provides clean hand speed, precise punch placement feedback, and adequate foam density for controlled heavy bag training and technical sparring.
* **16 oz Gloves:** Recommended if the primary objective is pure heavy bag pounding. The additional 2 ounces consists almost entirely of denser protective padding over the knuckles, preventing bruised knuckles and cartilage degradation over years of heavy training.
